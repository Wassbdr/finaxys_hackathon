# The problem: manipulation fraud in France (Banque de France, +34 %)

Research date: 2026-10-23. Main source: the OSMP 2025 annual report itself (132 pages, September 2026, published with the press release of 7 Sep 2026), read in full on manipulation fraud (sections 1.4, 1.7, 2.1 and the Interior ministry box). "Unverified" marks a fact read only in a secondary source.

- Report: https://www.banque-france.fr/fr/system/files/2026-09/Rapport_2025_de_l_OSMP.pdf
- Press release: https://www.banque-france.fr/system/files/2026-09/Communique_de_presse_du_rapport_2025.pdf

## Key facts

- **EUR 516m of manipulation fraud in 2025, +34 % in a year, 41.6 % of all payment fraud** (32.1 % in 2024, 21.6 % in 2021: the share doubled in four years). The most frequent form is the fake bank advisor. Total payment fraud: EUR 1.2bn (+3.8 %) over 7.2m fraudulent operations (-8 %). OSMP 2025 report, p. 4 and 24, Sep 2026.
- **How the OSMP counts it: fraud the victim authenticated.** The figure is a proxy: card payments made with strong customer authentication (EUR 140m, flat) plus online banking transfers (EUR 376m). The online banking part jumped **+54 %** (EUR 244m to 376m) while transfer flows grew 18 %; its fraud rate went from 0.0044 % to 0.0063 %. Series 2021 to 2025: 269, 340, 379, 384, 516 (EUR m). OSMP 2025 report, p. 24, Sep 2026.
- **The OSMP's own definition:** "the client is manipulated by the fraudster during a phone conversation, often impersonating the payment service provider (fake bank advisor or fake anti-fraud service)". Its goal "is to get the victim to validate fraudulent operations, through strong authentication, under the pretext of cancelling or blocking them". OSMP 2025 report, p. 24 and 29, Sep 2026.
- **Transfer fraud overall: EUR 485m, +37 %**, driven by online banking; fraudsters increasingly cash out through accounts opened in France (domestic transfer fraud rate +45 %). OSMP 2025 report, p. 21, Sep 2026.
- **Victims:** 448,300 victims of scams and payment fraud recorded by police and gendarmerie in 2025 (+7 %); 18 to 44 year olds are over 70 % of them; just over one victim in ten files a complaint; fake advisor complaints must be filed in person (not online via Thésée). Interior ministry box in the OSMP 2025 report, p. 25 to 27, Sep 2026.
- **What the OSMP asks of banks:** AI-based scoring with a wider data scope, "including phone metadata that tell whether the user is in a call (phone or video) when validating an atypical operation"; detect chains of risky operations (savings closed, new beneficiary, higher ceiling, then an unusual transfer); hold or block high-risk operations; show amount, payee and irrevocability at every authentication step with an explicit "refuse" option; use Verification of Payee and the national file of fraud-flagged accounts (live since May 2026). "A bank never needs its client to do anything to stop a payment suspected of fraud." OSMP 2025 report, p. 30 to 35, and press release, 7 Sep 2026.
- **Some French banking apps already check "call in progress"**, and the CNIL framed it on 15 Jul 2025: user consent required (beyond the Android permission), read only whether a call is ongoing and its duration, never listen or record, refusing must not block the app, retention 13 months. OSMP 2025 report, p. 35; https://www.cnil.fr/fr/controle-appel-en-cours-utilisation-dune-application-bancaire-reglementation (15 Jul 2025).

## How the scam works (2025)

1. **Data first.** Phishing SMS or mail, data leaks and social networks give the fraudster the victim's name, number, bank, sometimes the advisor's name; the OSMP says phishing data is "then reused in manipulation attempts" (report p. 29 to 30, 36).
2. **The contact.** A call showing the bank's or the advisor's own number (spoofing), or, since caller authentication, an ordinary 06 or 07 mobile number, WhatsApp or a social network (press release; report p. 34). Variant: an SMS about an "unusual payment" with a number to call back (secondary, unverified).
3. **The pretext.** "Suspicious transactions on your account": EUR 60 debits, payments in Romania or Africa. The caller sounds professional, keeps the victim on the line, creates urgency. One victim asked how to be sure: the caller told her to hang up and call the displayed number; she reached Crédit Mutuel's real customer service, which reassured her (franceinfo, 20 Jul 2026).
4. **The validation, by the real client, in the real app.** Presented as cancelling or securing: re-registering beneficiaries "to foil a cyberattack", a higher ceiling, a strongly authenticated card payment. The victim approves each step with her own strong authentication while still on the phone. In the BNP case judged by the Cour de cassation, the client re-added five beneficiaries with his code on his phone, on a call showing his own advisor's number, and lost EUR 54,500 (Cass. com., 23 Oct 2024, n° 23-16.267).
5. **Cash-out.** Transfers to mule accounts, more and more opened in France (report p. 21). Variant: a "courier" comes to collect the "compromised" card; fake couriers pushed up the card withdrawal fraud rate in 2025 (report p. 15 and 75).

**The direction matters:** the fraudster plays the bank and calls the client; the client is the one who signs. A fake client calling the bank to order a transfer is a different, smaller fraud, outside the EUR 516m proxy (that proxy counts strongly authenticated card payments and online banking transfers only).

## Why +34 % despite the measures

What the report puts forward (it gives no single cause for 2025):

- **Strong authentication moved the fraud onto the person.** After PSD2 strong customer authentication, card fraud hit its lowest rate ever (EUR 47 per EUR 100,000) and "fraudsters now favour manipulating their victim" (report p. 29). SCA is not a defence here: the victim performs it.
- **Online banking is the target:** +54 % in value against +18 % in flows (p. 24). Fraudsters combine phishing to get access and manipulation to get the validation (p. 21).
- **Caller authentication (MAN) shifted the channel, it did not end the calls.** The law requires operators to authenticate French caller numbers; unauthenticated calls to fixed lines are cut since 1 Oct 2024 and to mobiles since Jan 2025 (Selectra, unverified on arcep.fr); the OSMP says MAN is "largely operational since January 2025" (p. 34). Fraudsters moved to plain 06/07 numbers and messaging apps (press release). Spoofing persists through foreign routes: unauthenticated French mobile numbers calling from abroad are shown as hidden only since 1 Jan 2026, and the Do Not Originate trial for inbound-only bank numbers started in June 2026 (p. 34 to 35). Spoofing reports to Arcep rose from about 8,500 in 2024 to over 19,000 in 2025 (Siècle Digital, 16 Feb 2026, unverified on arcep.fr): https://siecledigital.fr/2026/02/16/appels-frauduleux-larcep-alerte-sur-une-explosion-des-signalements/
- **Verification of Payee came late in the year.** Mandatory since 9 Oct 2025 (p. 33), so it covers only the last quarter of 2025. Our reading, not the OSMP's: it checks that the name matches the IBAN, which does not help when the victim is coached to accept a mismatch or when the mule account is in a real name.
- **Instant transfers are not the culprit per the OSMP:** one transfer in six is instant and flows doubled, but the instant transfer fraud rate fell (0.046 % to 0.041 %) and stays below the card's (p. 4, 21).
- **The manipulation itself:** "techniques are more and more sophisticated and tend to alter the user's ability to take in the messages sent to them"; banks must "protect their clients despite themselves" (p. 29).

## Who pays

- **The law:** an unauthorised payment is refunded by the bank (Code monétaire et financier L. 133-18) unless the bank proves the client's gross negligence (L. 133-19, L. 133-23).
- **The Cour de cassation sides with the victim:** "No gross negligence ... can be attributed to an account holder who, called by someone posing as an employee of his bank whose number was displayed, uses the personalised security device at his request to delete and re-register transfer beneficiaries." BNP Paribas was ordered to refund EUR 54,500 plus EUR 1,500 damages. Cass. com., 23 Oct 2024, n° 23-16.267, published in the Bulletin: https://www.courdecassation.fr/decision/67189203d8ceca1cd7018c82 . The judges of fact still decide case by case. Extension to business clients: Cass. com., 12 Jun 2025, n° 24-13.777 (secondary, unverified): https://consultation.avocat.fr/blog/thomas-gauriat/article-2979100-fraude-au-faux-conseiller-bancaire-la-protection-des-clients-professionnels.html
- **The OSMP's refund rules (16 May 2023):** no strong authentication: refund at once; strong authentication: a first analysis within one business day, refund if there is doubt on consent, refuse only with proof of gross negligence. Its 2025 report says refunds of fraudulent transfers still need effort; the Banque de France and the ACPR now collect refund statistics bank by bank (report p. 30 to 32).
- **In practice banks still refuse:** Marie, 73, got nothing ("you gave your bank details"); Florence got half of EUR 4,000 back after a month and gave up; a lawyer puts a court case at 1.5 to 3 years (franceinfo, 20 Jul 2026).
- **EU: the bank will pay by default.** The PSR deal of 27 Nov 2025 makes the bank refund in full when a fraudster impersonates it and tricks the client into approving a payment, if the client reports to the police and the bank, unless fraud or gross negligence; platforms that leave reported fraud content online become liable to the refunding bank. ECON committee approved the text on 5 May 2026; plenary vote, Council adoption and Official Journal "expected by December 2026", general application about 21 months later, so around late 2028 (Adyen, updated 12 Oct 2026, unverified on EUR-Lex). Sources: https://www.europarl.europa.eu/news/en/press-room/20251121IPR31540/payment-services-deal-more-protection-from-online-fraud-and-hidden-fees , https://www.adyen.com/knowledge-hub/psd3

## Voice cloning

- **No figure exists.** The OSMP 2025 report never counts AI-generated voices in fake advisor calls. Its only mention is conditional and about companies: AI tools "producing perfectly forged emails, or voices or images impersonating company heads, could contribute" to CEO transfer fraud (Interior ministry box, p. 26). franceinfo adds there is no official count of fake advisor victims at all (20 Jul 2026).
- **Why it is rare in this scam (our reading):** the fake advisor plays a stranger, the victim does not know the voice; the levers are the displayed number, the data and the urgency. A clone matters when the voice is known: the CEO, a relative, one's own advisor.
- **What exists:** lawyers' and blog pages from 2026 describe fake advisor calls with cloned voices, without numbers (e.g. https://kohenavocats.com/voix-clonee-arnaque-faux-conseiller-virement-bloquer-reparation-2026/); vendor figures are US call centres (Pindrop, see [branch 3](branches/03-voice-deepfakes.md)).
- **Say on stage:** "AI makes these calls cheaper and more convincing", never "X % use cloned voices".

## Pitch hook

Marie is 73, retired, and lives in a village in the Bouches-du-Rhône. She is driving in Marseille to pick up her granddaughter from school, late, "20 cars behind me honking". A man with "a very cordial voice" calls: "This is your bank's head office", "you have already been charged EUR 60 six or eight times". It had happened to her before, so she answers: "do what's necessary". He then guides her through "a series of operations on the banking app". She loses EUR 23,000: "what was left of my parents' inheritance, I wanted to give it to my children". Her bank: "I would get nothing because I had given my bank details. I was responsible." (franceinfo, Valentine Joubin, 20 Jul 2026: https://www.franceinfo.fr/replay-radio/le-choix-franceinfo/temoignages-vous-n-obtiendrez-aucun-remboursement-le-difficile-combat-pour-etre-indemnise-apres-une-arnaque-au-faux-conseiller-bancaire_8084705.html)

The article does not say which operations she made in the app nor how the money left. For the mechanism, use the court record: a BNP client was told by "his advisor's assistant", on a call showing his advisor's number, that his transfer beneficiaries had been deleted "to foil a cyberattack" and had to be re-registered; staying on the line, he got messages on his phone asking him to validate beneficiary additions and entered his code each time; five beneficiaries were added and EUR 54,500 left his account (Cass. com., 23 Oct 2024, n° 23-16.267).

## 3 figures for the problem slide

1. **EUR 516m** lost to manipulation fraud in France in 2025, **+34 % in one year**, 41.6 % of all payment fraud (Banque de France, OSMP 2025 report, 7 Sep 2026).
2. **+54 %** on online banking transfers alone (EUR 244m to 376m) while flows grew 18 %: the victim approves the fraud herself, with strong authentication (same report, p. 24).
3. **EUR 54,500** that BNP Paribas had to refund to a client who validated the fraud in his own app: no gross negligence when the call shows the bank's number (Cour de cassation, 23 Oct 2024); the EU payments deal makes this refund the default.

## Consequence for our product

- In this scam the signer is the real client. A transaction-bound passkey on the real client's phone is strong authentication again: the client on the line with the fraudster signs it, as the BNP client entered his code.
- What the regulator points at fits the same build, turned around: the bank proves its calls to the client ("is it really my bank calling?", as Monzo's Call Status has done since Sep 2023: https://www.fintechfutures.com/digital-banking/monzo-announces-new-feature-to-help-protect-customers-against-impersonation-scams), and a validation made during a call the bank did not sign, after a chain of risky operations, is held and explained in plain words (call in progress per the OSMP and the CNIL, no listening).
